# artpipe

Procedural pixel-art pipeline (roadmap §6, milestone M4). Python 3, Pillow, numpy.

```
python tools/artpipe/build_theme.py Port     # generate sprites + .meta, then QA
python tools/artpipe/build_theme.py Port --props   # review sheet of the furnishing props: content-work/Port/props.png
python tools/artpipe/qa.py Port              # QA only; exit 1 on failure
python tools/artpipe/normalize.py in.png out.png --theme Port --size 32x32 --outline --trim --pivot bottom
python tools/artpipe/palette.py              # print the master palette
```

- `palette.py`: master palette (83 colours, 20 ramps: materials, accents, one backdrop haze ramp and one sky ramp per sky, dark to light). Step 0 is the outline/mortar colour; a sky ramp's step 0 is the camera's clear colour. Light comes from the top-left. The shared palette is what makes the three themes read as one game.
- `themes/<theme>.json`: the style. It lists the ramps the theme may use (`ramps` + `accents`), the seed, the sky and backdrop tint suggestions, per-material ramp and style choices, and the props' colour roles (`materials.props`). A material with a `variants` list draws its extra sprites (`chimney_1`, `bush_1`, `interior_1`...) with that entry laid over the base look.
- `backdrops.py`: the sky and three parallax layers per theme. `backdrop_sky` (512x384) is an ordered-dithered gradient whose top is the exact clear colour, so the camera can zoom out past it, with cumulus (Town), stratus bands and gulls (Port) or stars and wisps (Neon); `backdrop_sun` (64x64) is the sun or moon, one sprite glued to the camera so it never repeats. `backdrop_far/mid/near.png` (512x192) are seamless left/right silhouettes in the theme's haze ramp, with no outline; farther layers sit lighter and closer to the sky. A theme JSON can limit what it builds with `sprites` (Town keeps its hand-drawn trusses, glass columns and supports). `seeds` overrides the seed of single sprites (`{"wood": 6304}`) to re-roll one without changing the rest.
- `tiles.py`: one generator per sprite, plus `SPRITES` (size, tile axes, foreground). The same seed always gives the same pixels. Tileable sprites draw with wrap-around, and their joints sit away from the tile edge (brick courses start on it, so the corners line up).
- Building corners: every wall material also comes as `<name>_l` / `<name>_r` (`brick_l`, `brick_l_1`, `brick_tall_r`, `concrete_l`, `wood_r`...), the same tile with the theme's corner trim on its outer 6 px (`edge` in the JSON: `quoin` stones, a steel `post`, a neon `pilaster`; wood always gets a corner board, concrete a plain pilaster). `BuildingTileset.Dress` picks them for blocks on the building's silhouette (`BuildingSpec.EdgeOf`). Walls: `brick` (pillow bricks, the object-set look), `block`, `panel` (precast, two per tile), `corrugated`; `concrete_1` has a vent grille; `glass_1` has blinds and `glass_2` is boarded up; `chimney_1`, `chimney_2` are the theme's roof props (water tank, AC unit, vent cowl, sign box). `steel` is a column of plated wall one cell wide, tiling upwards: dark riveted plates, tougher at a glance than any masonry (`stripe`: a hazard band); `pole` a scaffold pole up the middle of its cell with a coupler and ledger stubs (`style` `clamp`, or `rope` for a lashed wooden one); `ladder` two rails and rungs, 20 px wide, with no outline (it is drawn behind the blocks) but a lit and a dark side so it reads on the back wall and against the sky.
- `build_theme.py`: writes `Demolition Men/Assets/Content/<Theme>/Sprites/*.png`. It adds a `.meta` (from `sprite.meta.template`: single sprite, 32 PPU, point filter, no compression) and folder metas only where none exist, so GUIDs survive regeneration. A PNG whose pixels are already the generated ones is not rewritten: PNG encoders differ between machines, and git would show every sprite as changed.
- What a theme builds (`theme_sprites`): its `sprites` list, or every block, tile and backdrop; on top of that the props its archetypes name; and the lit overlay of each of those that has one.
- `interior` / `interior_top`: a building's back wall behind open rooms and windows (`BuildingInterior` in the game), and the same under a ceiling with its shadow. Background: no outline, darker than the walls, so trusses and supports (the weak points) stand out. Styles `wallpaper`, `blocks`, `boards`, `plaster` (over a wainscot), `tiles`; `interior_1`, `interior_2` (+ `_top`) are the looks the other storeys get.
- Furnishing, `prop_<name>`: what stands in a building's rooms and on its roof. The game places props by name (`Furnishing.cs` is the catalog; `PROPS` in `tiles.py` mirrors it and `build_theme.py` stops when the two disagree), 32 px per cell, centre pivot: floor props stand on the sprite's bottom edge (`bar`, `djdesk`, `counter`, `desk`, `sofa`, `bed`, `table`, `workbench`, `pallet` 2x1; `speaker`, `fridge`, `rack`, `cabinet`, `slot`, `filing`, `racking`, `tank` 1x2; `machine`, `vault` 2x2; `plant` 1x1), wall props sit in the middle of their cell (`poster`, `picture`, `clock`, `shelf`, `pipe`, `board`, `tv` 1x1; `neon`, `bottles` 2x1), ceiling props hang from the top edge (`lamp`, `discoball`, `spot` 1x1; `tube` 2x1), roof signs stand on the bottom edge (`prop_sign_<word>` 2x1 for the words in `SIGN_WORDS`, set in the 5x7 `FONT` with doubled stems; six letters fit). Background: no outline, palette ramps only. A theme builds the props its archetypes name (`furnish.any` / `ground` / `upper` / `roof` in `Assets/Content/<Theme>/Archetypes/*.json`); an archetype naming one without a drawing stops the build. Their colours are roles in the theme JSON, `materials.props`: `wood`, `metal` (painted steel), `white` (paper, enamel, chrome), `fabric`, `screen` (black plastic and dark glass), `glow` (screens, fluorescent light), `light` (lamps, lit windows), `accent`, `accent2`, `plant`. A role is a ramp name (its top four steps as ink, shade, body, highlight) or four `[ramp, step]` pairs. The six wall things of the old room details (`poster` to `board`) keep their look: the back wall's line colour plus `materials.interior.accent`.
- Lit overlays, `<name>_lit`: the same size as their sprite and only its pixels that glow, in the brightest steps of `glow`, `light` and the accents. The game draws them unlit over the sprite at night and takes them away when the building loses power, so a prop's own sprite looks switched off (dark screen, grey tube, dull bulb). An overlay rolls the same random numbers as its sprite (`generate` seeds both from the sprite's name). Props: `bar`, `djdesk`, `fridge`, `cabinet`, `slot`, `desk`, `machine`, `vault`, `tv`, `neon`, `bottles`, `lamp`, `tube`, `discoball`, `spot` and every sign. Blocks, cut from the block's own pixels: `glass_lit`, `glass_1_lit` (the panes in `light`; the frame and the blinds' slats stay out), `door_lit` (a window or neon sign; a shutter has none), `chimney_lit`, `chimney_1_lit`... (a sign box, or a beacon). A block overlay that would be empty is not built.
- `build_theme.py <Theme> --props` writes `content-work/<Theme>/props.png` and nothing in Assets: the whole catalog in the theme's colours at 4x on its back wall, off beside lit, then everything lit at 2x on each of its three back walls. Props the theme's archetypes don't use are marked "not built".
- Street props (breakable in the game: `crate`, `car`, `barrel` (a gas tank), `container`) and dressing (cosmetic: `lamp`, `fence` (tiles along x), `bush`, `bin`, `sign`, plus variants `bush_1`, `bin_1`, `sign_1`, `sign_2`). Each theme picks their look in its JSON (`fence` style `picket`/`chain`/`rail`, `bush` style `hedge`/`tires`/`bags`/`pallets`, `bin` style `can`/`bollard`/`hydrant`/`postbox`/`cone`, `sign` plain or `neon` with `faces` `symbol`/`arrow`/`stop`/`p`). `unity_theme.py` writes the props into the tileset and the dressing into the theme's `decor`.
- Ground: `ground_top` (`dock` planks, a `kerb`, or `pavement` slabs) over `ground_fill` (`blocks`, `asphalt` or `soil` with stones).
- `unity_theme.py <Theme>` writes the tiles, tileset and theme assets from the sprite files (naming: `<kind>`, `<kind>_1`... variants; `<kind>_l`, `<kind>_r` corners; `interior_1`...; `prop_<name>`; `<sprite>_lit` overlays; `ladder`; decor variants). An existing theme asset keeps the fields the script doesn't own (lights, music); a tileset entry the theme doesn't generate (Town's hand-drawn glass, trusses, supports) is kept as it is.
- `level_preview.py`: draws generated levels with the theme's real assets, headless. `tools/unity-check/check.sh levels [id...]` writes the layouts to `content-work/levels/*.json`; `python3 tools/artpipe/level_preview.py [id...]` draws `content-work/levels/<id>.png` (the level cut into stacked strips; backdrops without parallax). The X on the weak points of a level's first buildings (`weakMarks` in its spec, `mark` in the layout) is drawn too.
- `marks.py`: the paint the game sprays on blocks, in `Demolition Men/Assets/Resources/Marks/`: `WeakPoint.png`, the X on a weak point (`WeakPointMark`; docs/hit-plan.md F1). One cell (32x32), imported like the block sprites it sits on (32 PPU, point filter, centred pivot); hazard yellow inside the outline colour, so it reads on brick, wood, steel and the dark Neon walls. Review sheet: `content-work/marks.png`, the X on real blocks of every theme at 4x and 2x.
- `ui.py`: the shared UI sprites in `Demolition Men/Assets/Resources/UI/` (riveted plate for 9-sliced buttons and panels, hazard stripe, star). They are light grey so the Image colour tints them, and are imported at 25 pixels per unit (4 UI units per pixel).
- `action_frames.py`: the Actions' poses in `content-work/actions/<action>/` (order in its `ACTIONS`) to `Demolition Men/Assets/Resources/Actions/P1|P2/<action>_<n>.png` with `.meta` (pivot on the belt, bottom edge, like the player sheets; an existing GUID is kept), Player 2's in its colours with its stop sign turned as far as the warning sign leans; plus the props `pallet` (drawn), `radio` (cut from the groove pose) and `note`.
- `player_fit.py in.png out.png [--scale k] [--sign-border]`: fits a generated player pose (the Actions' keyframes in `content-work/actions/`) to the hand-drawn player sheet: the character snaps to the sheet's 18 colours, props to the material and accent ramps, alpha goes hard; `--scale` box-shrinks a render whose sign is wider than the sheet's 14 px, `--sign-border` adds the sign's white ring.
- `brand.py`: store and brand art from the players' sprites, the object set, the Town backdrops and the title font. Play icon (`brand/icon-512.png`), feature graphic (`brand/feature-graphic.png`, 1024x500, no alpha), the logo on one and two lines (`brand/logo*.png`, transparent), and the app icon in `Demolition Men/Assets/Brand/` (`Icon` for default/legacy/round, `IconBackground` + `IconForeground` for the adaptive icon). Preview with launcher masks: `content-work/brand.png`.
- `qa.py` checks:
  - every colour is in the theme palette;
  - alpha is hard;
  - each sprite has its exact expected size, a multiple of 32;
  - each sprite has a `.meta`;
  - seams are continuous on each tile axis.

  It writes `content-work/<Theme>/contact.png`. The sheet shows the sprites at 4x, labelled, beside the reference object set. It also has tiled previews and a mock street on the theme sky.

To add a theme, copy a theme JSON, change its ramps, materials and seed, run `build_theme.py` and look at the contact sheet.
